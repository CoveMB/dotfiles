# Recurring coordination monitors

- Before creating or materially changing a monitor, verify current native scheduling capabilities. Define the active cadence, consecutive unchanged-check threshold, quiet intervals and cap, reset/recovery conditions, and retry/deadline handling. State expected detection delay assuming normal runs, separately from queue/runtime uncertainty and documented guarantees.
- Qualify quiet waits through fresh owner, runtime and checkpoint evidence with no eligible work or handoff. Controller idleness or notification silence does not establish inactivity. Missing/stale evidence or inspection failure earns no quiet credit; retain timely coordination while owners are active.
- Backoff must reduce scheduled model wakeups. Notification suppression or prompt shortening alone is insufficient. If adaptive changes are unsupported, propose a supported fixed cadence with its limitations; do not build a scheduler.
- Require explicit approval for the chosen schedule and the precise conditions/bounds of future automatic schedule changes; reuse existing approval. Preserve ownership, pending decisions, notification preferences and completion rules; do not automatically reactivate paused/completed monitors.
- Keep prompts compact, retaining essential authority boundaries and mandatory reads of verified authoritative checkpoints. Reuse existing state for decisions/history instead of repeatedly appending historical narrative or creating another controller.
- Use proportionate local verification. Observe the first actual backoff and reset during ordinary authorized runs; do not trigger implementation or create extra monitoring solely to test them. Until observed, distinguish policy simulation and saved configuration from scheduler/model behavior and actual wakeup/token savings.
